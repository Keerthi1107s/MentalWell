About the project

Understanding mental health starts with a conversation.

Mental health is part of our overall health. It shapes how we think, feel, connect with others, and move through everyday life. Everyone can experience changes in emotional well-being, and asking for support is a normal, positive step.

MentalWell is designed to improve awareness and make trusted support information easier to discover. It is for students, young adults, families, and anyone looking for a place to begin.

Student well-being

College is a lot to carry. You don’t have to carry it alone.

Academic pressure, career uncertainty, social anxiety, homesickness, friendships, relationships, sleep, burnout, and expectations can all affect student well-being.

Exam stressHomesicknessBurnoutFriendshipsCareer uncertainty

During exam season

  • Break study into short, realistic sessions.
  • Plan small pauses, meals, water, and sleep.
  • Talk to someone you trust when pressure builds.
  • Ask your campus for academic or counselling support.
  • Remember: one exam does not define your worth.

Mental Health: Myths vs Facts

Clear information can make it easier to listen, understand, and reach out.

Myth

“Only people with serious problems need mental health support.”

Fact

Anyone can benefit from support during a difficult period. You don’t need to wait until things feel overwhelming.

Myth

“Talking about mental health makes things worse.”

Fact

Open, supportive conversations can reduce stigma and help people find appropriate support.

Myth

“Taking a break means you are falling behind.”

Fact

Rest is part of caring for yourself. A pause can help you return with more energy.

Myth

“People should handle everything on their own.”

Fact

Reaching out to someone you trust or a professional is a healthy way to seek support.
